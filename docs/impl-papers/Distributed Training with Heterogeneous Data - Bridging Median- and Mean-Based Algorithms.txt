2. PROBLEM SETUP / WHAT THE PAPER IS SOLVING
================================================================================
The paper studies distributed/federated training in the parameter-server
paradigm, where the goal is to minimize f(x) = (1/M) * sum_{i=1}^{M} f_i(x)
across M worker nodes, each with its own local objective f_i (Eq. 1), with
f_i defined either as an expectation over a local data distribution p_i(zeta)
or as an empirical average over K_i local samples (Eq. 2).

Two "median-based" distributed algorithms exist in the literature: SIGNSGD
with majority vote (communication-efficient, 1-bit gradient compression) and
MEDIANSGD / MEDIANGD (coordinate-wise median aggregation, originally proposed
for Byzantine robustness). Both were previously analyzed only under the
assumption that local data/gradients are i.i.d. across workers (homogeneous
data). The paper shows that under heterogeneous (non-i.i.d.) data, as in
Federated Learning, both algorithms fail to converge whenever there is a gap
between the expected median and expected mean of the local gradients across
workers. The paper's contribution is (a) proving SIGNSGD is itself a
median-based algorithm (it updates along the signed median direction, not a
literal sign-of-mean), (b) proving a non-convergence result under
heterogeneous data, and (c) proposing a noise-perturbation mechanism (adding
symmetric, unimodal noise to local gradients before aggregation) that
provably closes the median-mean gap and restores convergence to a stationary
point for both algorithms, now called Noisy SIGNSGD and Noisy MEDIANSGD.

3. ALGORITHM 2 — MEDIANSGD (paper calls the base algorithm "MEDIANSGD" in the
   algorithm box; also refers to it as MEDIANGD / "generalized stochastic
   version of MEDIANGD" when citing Yin et al. [28])
================================================================================
Pseudocode as given verbatim (Algorithm 2, "MEDIANSGD (with M nodes)"):

  Input: learning rate delta, current point x_t
  1: g_{t,i} <- grad f_i(x_t) + sampling noise          (for each node i)
  2: x_{t+1} <- x_t - delta * median({g_{t,i}}_{i=1}^{M})

Notation: for a set of vectors {a_i}_{i=1}^n, median({a_i}) denotes the
COORDINATE-WISE median of the vectors (i.e., for each coordinate j, take the
median across the M nodes' values at coordinate j). sign(v) is the signed
(coordinate-wise) vector; (v)_j denotes the jth coordinate of v.

Assumptions used in the analysis (stated as A1-A4, apply to both algorithms):
  A1. Unbiased gradient estimator: E[g_i(x)] = grad f_i(x).
  A2. Bounded variance: E[ || (median({g_t}))_j - E[(median({g_t}))_j | x_t] ||^2 ] <= sigma_m^2, for all j in [d].
      (A variant of the classical bounded-variance assumption, applied to the
      median rather than individual gradients; satisfied if all g_{t,i} have
      bounded variance.)
  A3. f has Lipschitz gradient: || grad f(x) - grad f(y) || <= L ||x - y||.
  A4. M is an odd number (ensures the coordinate-wise median is well defined).

Theorem 2 (convergence of plain/un-noised MEDIANSGD under A1-A4):
Suppose A1-A4 hold. Define D_f := f(x_1) - min_x f(x). Set
  delta = min( 1/sqrt(T*d), 1/(2L) ).
Then MEDIANSGD yields:

  (1/T) * sum_{t=1}^{T} E[ ||grad f(x_t)||^2 ]
    <= (2*sqrt(2)*sqrt(d)/sqrt(T)) * D_f
       + 2L*sqrt(d)*sigma_m^2
       + (1/T) * sum_{t=1}^{T} E[ || grad f(x_t) - E[median({g_t}) | x_t] ||^2 ]

(This is Eq. (5) in the paper.) As T -> infinity, the RHS is dominated by the
last term, i.e. the squared gap between the true gradient and the expected
coordinate-wise median of local gradients. If local gradients at different
nodes are drawn from the same symmetric, unimodal distribution (homogeneous
case) this gap term vanishes and the algorithm converges to a stationary
point at rate O(sqrt(d)/sqrt(T)). Under heterogeneous data (gap nonzero), the
paper's Theorem 3 shows this gap is NOT just a proof artifact: there provably
exist problem instances where MEDIANSGD converges only to a point with
||grad f(x_hat*)||^2 >= (1/T) sum_t E[||E[median({g_t})] - grad f(x_t)||^2] >= 1,
i.e., a constant lower bound on the limiting gradient norm proportional to the
median-mean gap, so plain MEDIANSGD does NOT converge to a true stationary
point when data is heterogeneous.

Noisy version — Algorithm 4, "Noisy MEDIANSGD":
  Input: learning rate delta, current point x_t
  1: g_{t,i} <- g_hat_{t,i} + b * xi_{t,i}
  2: x_{t+1} <- x_t - delta * median({g_{t,i}}_{i=1}^{M})

Here g_hat_{t,i} is the original (possibly stochastic) local gradient
estimator, b is a scalar controlling the noise magnitude, and xi_{t,i} is
sampled i.i.d. (per coordinate) from a symmetric, unimodal distribution with
mean 0 and variance 1 and pdf h(z) (this is the "noise model" — see Section 3
below for the general noise-closing-the-gap theorem, Theorem 4, that
motivates this). The only change versus the un-noised Algorithm 2 is adding
noise to the local gradient before the median/aggregation step.

Convergence guarantee for Noisy MEDIANSGD — Theorem 6:
Suppose A1, A3, A4 are satisfied and |(g_hat_{t,i})_j| <= Q for all t, j
(bounded raw gradient coordinates). Each coordinate of xi_{t,i} is sampled
i.i.d. from a symmetric, unimodal distribution with mean 0, variance 1, pdf
h(z); h'(z) is uniformly bounded and absolutely integrable. Define
D_f := f(x_1) - min_x f(x). Set delta <= 1/(2L). Then:

  (1/T) * sum_{t=1}^{T} E[ ||grad f(x_t)||^2 ] <= (2/(T*delta)) * D_f
       + O(d/b^2) + O(delta * d * b^2)

(Eq. 12.) This shows a bias-variance-type trade-off in b: larger b shrinks
the median-mean gap term O(d/b^2) but inflates the noise-injection term
O(delta*d*b^2). The paper states the best achievable convergence rate under
this trade-off is O(d^{2/3} / T^{1/3}), obtained by setting
b = T^{1/6} * d^{1/6} and delta = 1/(T^{2/3} * d^{2/3}).
The paper notes MEDIANSGD's robustness (resistance to extreme/outlier
values, e.g. Byzantine workers) comes from using the median rather than mean;
as more noise b is added, the median estimator is "gradually converting...to
a mean estimator," which should make MEDIANSGD progressively LESS robust to
Byzantine/outlier effects even as it converges better under heterogeneity. No
closed-form numerical constants are given for the O(.) terms in Theorem 6 in
the main text (constants presumably detailed in the appendix, which is not
included in this PDF).

4. ALGORITHM 3 — SIGNSGD (WITH MAJORITY VOTE) and its noisy variant
   (the user's file is named signsgd.py; in this paper SIGNSGD with majority
   vote is "Algorithm 1," not "Algorithm 3" — the noisy version of SIGNSGD is

================================================================================
NOTE ON ALGORITHM NUMBERING: In this paper, the four algorithm boxes are:
  Algorithm 1 = SIGNSGD (with M nodes) [plain/base]
  Algorithm 2 = MEDIANSGD (with M nodes) [plain/base]
  Algorithm 3 = Noisy SIGNSGD
  Algorithm 4 = Noisy MEDIANSGD
This differs from the numbering implied in the task prompt (which called
noisy signSGD "Algorithm 3" as if it were the base algorithm); in the actual
paper, "Algorithm 3" is specifically the NOISY SIGNSGD variant, and plain
SIGNSGD with majority vote is Algorithm 1. Both the plain and noisy versions
are transcribed below for completeness since both are implementation-relevant.

Pseudocode, plain SIGNSGD — Algorithm 1, "SIGNSGD (with M nodes)":
  Input: learning rate delta, current point x_t
  1: g_{t,i} <- grad f_i(x_t) + sampling noise          (for each node i)
  2: x_{t+1} <- x_t - delta * sign( sum_{i=1}^{M} sign(g_{t,i}) )

This is literally a majority vote: each node sends only the coordinate-wise
sign of its local (stochastic) gradient; the server sums the M sign vectors
and takes the sign of that sum (i.e., majority vote per coordinate), then
takes a step of fixed size delta in that direction. This is the source of
SIGNSGD's communication efficiency: only 1-bit-per-coordinate gradient
information needs to be sent per node per round (paper states this gives up
to 16x or 32x communication reduction versus plain SGD in the homogeneous
MNIST experiment, see Section 5 below).

Key structural result connecting SIGNSGD to the median — Proposition 1:
"When M is odd and median({g_t}) != 0, we have
  sign( sum_{i=1}^{M} sign(g_{t,i}) ) = sign( median({g_t}) )."
(Eq. 3.) I.e., majority-vote-of-signs is mathematically identical to the sign
of the coordinate-wise median of the raw gradients (when M is odd and the
median is nonzero). This is presented as the paper's key structural
observation ("SIGNSGD as a median-based algorithm") that lets it analyze
SIGNSGD and MEDIANSGD under one unified median-based framework.

Convergence guarantee for plain SIGNSGD — Theorem 1:
Suppose A1-A4 are satisfied, and define D_f := f(x_1) - min_x f(x). For
SIGNSGD with delta = sqrt(D_f) / sqrt(L*d*T), the following holds:

  (1/T) * sum_{t=1}^{T} E[ ||grad f(x_t)||_1 ]
    <= (3/2) * sqrt(d*L*D_f) / sqrt(T)
       + 2*d*sigma_m
       + 2 * (1/T) * sum_{t=1}^{T} E[ || E[median({g_t}) | x_t] - grad f(x_t) ||_1 ]

(Eq. 4.) As T -> infinity, RHS is dominated by the difference between the
median and mean of gradients, plus the standard deviation sigma_m on the
median. If gradient estimators across nodes are drawn from the SAME unimodal,
symmetric distribution (the homogeneous assumption of prior work, e.g.
Bernstein et al. [4]), this recovers the bound in Bernstein et al. [4]: in
that case E[median({g_t})|x_t] = E[grad f(x_t)] and sigma_m = O(sigma_l /
sqrt(M)) if the per-coordinate noise on each local gradient has variance
bounded by sigma_l^2 (citing Theorem 1.4.1 in Miller [19], "The Probability
Lifesaver: Order Statistics and the Median Theorem"). In that homogeneous
case, Eq. 4 becomes:
  (1/T) sum_t E[||grad f(x_t)||_1] <= (3/2)*sqrt(d*L*D_f)/sqrt(T) + O(d/sqrt(M))
and using a larger minibatch to decrease sigma_l further improves convergence.

Under heterogeneous data (gap nonzero), Theorem 3 shows (companion to the
MEDIANSGD result above) that there exist problem instances where SIGNSGD
converges only to a point x_hat* with
  ||grad f(x_hat*)||_1 >= (1/T) sum_t E[||E[median({g_t})] - grad f(x_t)||_1] >= 1,
i.e. a nonzero, constant lower bound on the (L1) gradient norm at the point
SIGNSGD converges to — confirming SIGNSGD genuinely fails to reach a
stationary point under heterogeneous data, not merely as an analysis
artifact. (The paper gives a concrete 1-D 3-worker counterexample earlier in
Section 1: f(x) = (1/3) sum_{i=1}^3 (x - a_i)^2 / 2 with a_1=1, a_2=2, a_3=10,
step size delta=0.001, initial point x_0=0.0005, run for up to 10,000
iterations — Figure 1 shows both SIGNSGD and MEDIANSGD driving the MEDIAN of
gradients to 0 while the TRUE gradient/mean stays roughly constant /
nonzero.)

Noisy SIGNSGD — Algorithm 3, "Noisy SIGNSGD":
  Input: learning rate delta, current point x_t
  1: g_{t,i} <- g_hat_{t,i} + b * xi_{t,i}
  2: x_{t+1} <- x_t - delta * sign( sum_{i=1}^{M} sign(g_{t,i}) )

Same structure as plain SIGNSGD (majority vote of signs), except the local
gradient g_hat_{t,i} has artificial noise b*xi_{t,i} added before the sign is
taken. xi_{t,i} sampled i.i.d. (coordinate-wise) from a symmetric, unimodal
distribution with mean 0, variance 1, pdf h(z); b is a scalar noise-scale
parameter. (Paper notes this injected noise is similar in spirit to the noise
added for differential privacy — when a bound on gradient norm is known, a
standard privacy accountant could be used to compute the associated privacy
cost, though the paper explicitly leaves the DP utility analysis "to future
works.")

Convergence guarantee for Noisy SIGNSGD — Theorem 5:
Suppose A1, A3, A4 are satisfied and |(g_hat_{t,i})_j| <= Q for all t, j. Each
coordinate of xi_{t,i} sampled i.i.d. from a symmetric, unimodal distribution
with mean 0, variance 1, pdf h(z); h'(z) and h''(z) uniformly bounded and
absolutely integrable. Define sigma to be the standard deviation of the
median of 2n+1 samples drawn from h(z); D_f := f(x_1) - min_x f(x); W_t :=
the set of coordinates j at iteration t with |grad f(x_t)_j| / (b*sigma) >=
2/sqrt(3). For SIGNSGD:

  (1/T) * sum_{t=1}^{T} ( sum_{j in W_t} |grad f(x_t)_j|
                           + sum_{j in [d]\W_t} (1/(b*sigma)) * grad f(x_t)_j^2 )
    <= 3*D_f / (T*delta)
       + 3/T * sum_{t=1}^{T} E[ sum_{j=1}^{d} |grad f(x_t)_j| * O(1/b^2) ]
       + (3*L/2) * delta * d

(Eq. 11.) Effect of b: the term that decreases with b on the RHS is the
convergence gap introduced by heterogeneous data / data sampling; without
artificial noise this term is a constant (does not vanish), confirming noise
provably helps. The other two RHS terms are standard and diminish with proper
choice of T and delta — reflecting a trade-off between convergence speed
(worse with larger b) and final accuracy (better with larger b). Optimizing b
and delta jointly, the paper obtains a worst-case bound:
  (1/T) sum_t ( sum_{j in W_t} T^{1/4} d^{1/4} |grad f(x_t)_j|
                + sum_{j in [d]\W_t} grad f(x_t)_j^2 )  <=  O( d^{3/4} / T^{1/4} )
by setting b = T^{1/4} * d^{1/4} and delta = 1/sqrt(T*d). This rate is
explicitly noted to be SLOWER than plain SGD's O(sqrt(d)/sqrt(T)) rate,
implying "SIGNSGD may not be preferred when data heterogeneity is large."
The paper also contrasts its fix with the error-feedback fix of SIGNSGD in
Karimireddy et al. [13]: the noise approach here addresses the
multi-worker/heterogeneity setting without modifying SIGNSGD's update rule,
whereas error feedback is designed for the single-worker setting and uses
gradient-magnitude information in parameter updates (i.e., the two fixes
target different failure modes and are not directly substitutable).

GENERAL NOISE-CLOSES-THE-GAP RESULT (Theorem 4, Section 3) underlying both
noisy algorithms:
Given 2n+1 numbers u_1,...,u_{2n+1}, a symmetric unimodal noise distribution
with mean 0, variance 1, pdf h_0(z), cdf H_0(z) (h_0'(z) uniformly bounded
and absolutely integrable). Draw 2n+1 i.i.d. samples xi_1,...,xi_{2n+1} from
h_0(z). Define u_hat_i := u_i + b*xi_i and u_bar := (1/(2n+1)) sum u_i [the
paper writes u_bar as sum_{i=1}^{2n+1} u_i, consistent with its notation
elsewhere using a normalized sum/mean].
(a) E[median({u_hat_i}_{i=1}^{2n+1})] = u_bar + O( max_{i,j} |u_i - u_j|^2 / b )
    — Eq. 6 — i.e. the expected median of the noise-perturbed values
    approaches the true mean u_bar at rate O(1/b) as b grows.
    Var(median({u_hat_i})) = O(b^2) — Eq. 7.
(b) Further assuming h_0''(z) is uniformly bounded and absolutely integrable,
    and denoting r_b(z) as the pdf of median({u_hat_i}), the pdf decomposes as
      r_b(u_bar + z) = (1/b) g(z/b)  [symmetric part]  +  (1/b) v(z/b)  [asymmetric part]
    (Eq. 8), where g(z) is the pdf of the sample median of 2n+1 draws from
    h_0(z) (symmetric about 0) given explicitly by Eq. 9 (a combinatorial sum
    over subsets S of [2n+1]\{i} of products of H_0(z) and H_0(-z) terms,
    weighted by h_0(z)), and the asymmetric part satisfies
      integral_{-inf}^{inf} (1/b)|v(z/b)| dz = O( max_i |u_bar - u_i|^2 / b^2 )  (Eq. 10).
    I.e. as b grows, the pdf of the median becomes increasingly symmetric
    (asymmetric part shrinks at rate O(1/b^2)), and the gap between expected
    median and true mean shrinks at rate O(1/b).
Gaussian noise is noted to satisfy all assumptions of Theorem 4. The paper
also states it observes EMPIRICALLY (not proven) that other common symmetric
distributions with non-differentiable points, such as Laplace and uniform
distributions, also appear to make the pdf increasingly symmetric and bring
the expected median closer to the mean as b grows.

6. OTHER IMPLEMENTATION-RELEVANT DETAILS
================================================================================
- Aggregation target: both algorithms aggregate GRADIENTS (or gradient
  estimators g_{t,i} = grad f_i(x_t) + noise), not full local model updates/
  weights. This is a gradient-aggregation scheme (SGD-style, synchronous,
  one step per round), not a FedAvg-style multi-local-step weight-averaging
  scheme. Each round: every node computes one (possibly noised) local
  gradient estimate, sends it (or its sign, for SIGNSGD) to the server, the
  server aggregates (coordinate-wise median, or majority vote of signs), and
  a single global step of size delta is taken.
- No momentum term appears anywhere in any of the four algorithm boxes
  (Algorithms 1-4) — these are vanilla (non-momentum) SGD-style updates.
- SIGNSGD communicates only the sign of each gradient coordinate (1 bit/
  coordinate/node/round) — this is the source of its "communication
  efficiency" framing (16x-32x reduction vs. plain SGD, per the homogeneous
  MNIST comparison citing Bernstein et al. [4]).
- MEDIANSGD communicates full (noised) gradient vectors — no compression —
  its design motivation is Byzantine robustness via coordinate-wise median
  (robust to outliers/extreme values), not communication efficiency. The
  paper notes a trade-off: adding more perturbation noise b improves
  convergence under heterogeneity but reduces Byzantine robustness, since it
  pushes the median estimator toward behaving like a mean estimator (which
  is NOT robust to outliers/Byzantine corruption).
- Baselines/prior work compared against or cited for context (not necessarily
  run as baselines in this paper's own experiments, except where noted):
  - Bernstein et al. [4,5] — original SIGNSGD / SIGNSGD-with-majority-vote
    papers, analyzed only under homogeneous-data / same-distribution
    assumptions; this paper's Theorem 1 homogeneous-case bound reduces to
    theirs.
  - Yin et al. [28] — original MEDIANGD, Byzantine-robust, optimal
    statistical rate, also assumed i.i.d. data across workers.
  - Karimireddy et al. [13] — "error feedback" fix for SIGNSGD, designed for
    single-worker (not multi-worker/heterogeneous) setting; explicitly
    contrasted with this paper's noise-based fix (different problem setting,
    not a head-to-head empirical comparison in this paper).
  - Plain SGD [22] (Robbins-Monro) — cited as the baseline communication/
    convergence-speed reference point for the homogeneous-data SIGNSGD
    comparison on CIFAR-10 (16x/32x communication reduction claim, from
    Bernstein et al. [4]'s result, not independently re-derived here).
  - Alistarh et al. [2] (Byzantine SGD), Blanchard et al. [6] (Krum) — cited
    in Related Work on Byzantine-robust optimization generally, not used as
    empirical baselines in this paper's own experiments.
- Noise model used in both noisy algorithms and in the theory (Theorem 4-6):
  noise xi_{t,i} (or xi_i) must be drawn i.i.d., coordinate-wise, from a
  distribution that is symmetric and unimodal, with mean 0 and variance 1;
  the noise is scaled by a scalar b before being added to the raw gradient
  (g = g_hat + b*xi). Differentiability conditions on the noise pdf (h'(z),
  h''(z) uniformly bounded and absolutely integrable) are required for the
  sharper theoretical results (Theorem 4b, Theorem 5, Theorem 6) but the
  paper notes empirically that even non-smooth symmetric distributions like
  Laplace and uniform seem to work similarly in practice (not proven,
  observed only).
- M (number of nodes) is required to be ODD for the median to be well
  defined and for Proposition 1 (SIGNSGD = sign of median) to hold exactly.
  This is a hard constraint worth preserving in any strategy implementation
  that wants the SIGNSGD-median equivalence to be exact.
- The paper explicitly states its scope: "the focus of this paper is NOT to
  analyze properties of median-based methods beyond convergence" — i.e., it
  does not re-derive or re-validate Byzantine-robustness guarantees for
  MEDIANSGD under the noise perturbation; that trade-off is discussed only
  qualitatively (end of Section 4).
