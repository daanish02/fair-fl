2. PROBLEM SETUP
-----------------
The paper addresses group fairness (demographic parity / equalized odds) in
federated learning, distinguishing LOCAL fairness (unbiased predictions
evaluated on each client's own local data distribution) from GLOBAL fairness
(unbiased predictions evaluated on the pooled distribution across all
clients). Under statistical heterogeneity across clients, the paper states
(citing Hamman and Dutta 2024, and its own Figure 1b illustration) that
neither local fairness implies global fairness nor vice versa — this is
"Challenge 1" (CH1) the paper sets out to solve. "Challenge 2" (CH2) is that
most prior fair-FL work uses in-processing (intervening during training),
which is costly to retrain, communication-heavy, and privacy-invasive; the
paper instead frames fairness as a POST-PROCESSING problem over an
already-trained probabilistic classifier, searching for a model-agnostic
calibration of a pre-trained federated model. The target is explicitly the
BAYES-OPTIMAL classifier under BOTH local and global fairness constraints
simultaneously — i.e., the classifier h that minimizes misclassification
risk R(h) subject to the local and global fairness-violation measures being
below given thresholds (delta_l, delta_g). This is claimed (Introduction,
Contributions) to be the first method offering both local and global
fairness guarantees in FL.

3. ALGORITHM
-------------
Setup / notation (Sec 3.1-3.2, page 22472-22473):
- Random tuple (X, A, Y): X in feature space R^d, Y in {0,1} (binary
  classification), sensitive attribute A in {-1, 1} (two sensitive groups,
  "without loss of generality... this paper concentrates on two sensitive
  groups").
- FL system: |C| clients, client c has local dataset D_c with samples
  (x_{c,i}, a_{c,i}, y_{c,i}), i in [N_c].
- Bayes score function extended to FL: eta(x,a,c) := P(Y=1 | X=x, A=a, C=c)
  — Eq. (1).
- Fairness metrics used: Demographic Parity (DP, Dwork et al. 2012):
  M_DP = |P(Yhat=1|A=-1) - P(Yhat=1|A=1)|.
  Equalized Odds (EO, Hardt, Price, Srebro 2016):
  M_EO = max_{y in {0,1}} |P(Yhat=1|A=-1,Y=y) - P(Yhat=1|A=1,Y=y)|.
- These are generalized via "composite linear disparity": for classifier
  h(x,a), M_phi(h) = max_{k=1..K} |D_k(h)|, where
  D_k(h) = sum_{a in A} E_{X~P^a_X}[phi^a_k(eta(X,a)) h(X,a)].
  Example 1 gives: K=1, phi^a_1 = a -> recovers DP. K=2 with
  phi^a_1(w) = a*w / P(Y=1|A=a), phi^a_2(w) = a(1-w) / P(Y=0|A=a) -> recovers
  EO. (This composite form also covers Equality of Opportunity and
  Predictive Equality per the text, though those are not separately
  evaluated in experiments.)
- Local vs global fairness formalized (Definition 1, page 22473-22474):
  Local fairness M^l,c_psi(h) = max over k_l of |D^l,c_{k_l}(h)|, where
  D^l,c_{k_l}(h) = sum_{a in A} E_{X~P^a_{X,c}}[psi^{a,c}_{k_l}(eta(X,a,c))
  h(X,a,c)] (evaluated on client c's local conditional distribution
  P(X,A,Y|C)).
  Global fairness M^g_phi(h) = max over k_g of |D^g_{k_g}(h)|, where
  D^g_{k_g}(h) = sum_{a in A} sum_{c in C} E_{X~P^a_{X,c}}
  [phi^{a,c}_{k_g}(eta(X,a,c)) h(X,a,c)] (evaluated on the global/pooled
  distribution P(X,A,Y)).
  {psi^{a,c}_{k_l}} and {phi^{a,c}_{k_g}} are linear functions depending on a
  and c; K^{l,c} and K^g are the number of local/global linear disparities
  needed to characterize the fairness criterion.

Assumption 1 (eta-continuity, page 22474): for each client c, the mapping
t -> P(eta(X,A,C) <= t | A=a, C=c) is almost surely continuous restricted to
[0,1] — i.e. the conditional-probability function has no atoms. The paper
states this is "of little or no concern in practice" and can be satisfied by
adding slight random noise to any estimator.

Optimization problem (Eq. 2, page 22474):
  min_{h in H} R(h)
  s.t. |D^{l,c}_{k_l}(h)| <= delta_{l,c}, for all c in [C], k_l in [K^{l,c}]
       |D^g_{k_g}(h)| <= delta_g, for all k_g in [K^g]
The solution is called the "Bayes optimal federated fair classifier" —
theoretically achieves the optimal accuracy/global-fairness/local-fairness
trade-off.

THEOREM 1 (Bayes optimal federated fair classifier, page 22474, "Theorem 1"):
Under Assumption 1, for p_{a,c} := P(A=a, C=c), phi^{a,c} := [phi^{a,c}_{k_g}]
for k_g=1..K^g, and psi^{a,c} := [psi^{a,c}_{k_l}] for k_l=1..K^{l,c}, h* is
the Bayes optimal federated fair classifier solving Problem (2) if:

  h*(x,a,c) = I[F(lambda*, mu*, x, a, c) >= 0]                        (no eq#
                                                                        given
                                                                        beyond
                                                                        stmt)

  F(lambda*, mu*, x, a, c)
    = p_{a,c} * (2*eta(x,a,c) - 1)
      - (lambda*_1 - lambda*_2)^T * phi^{a,c}(eta(x,a,c))
      - (mu*_{1,c} - mu*_{2,c})^T * psi^{a,c}(eta(x,a,c))                (3)

where (lambda*, mu*), with lambda = (lambda_1, lambda_2) in R^{2*K^g},
mu_c = (mu_{1,c}, mu_{2,c}) in R^{2*K^{l,c}}, mu = [mu_c]_{c in C}, and
lambda, mu >= 0, are the optimal "Lagrangian-style" dual parameters
determined by the convex minimization problem:

  (lambda*, mu*) in argmin_{lambda, mu >= 0} H(lambda, mu)               (no
                                                                        explicit
                                                                        eq# in
                                                                        extracted
                                                                        text,
                                                                        appears
                                                                        directly
                                                                        before
                                                                        Eq. 4)

  H(lambda, mu) = sum_{c in C} sum_{a in A} E_{X~P^a_{X,c}}
                    [ (F(lambda, mu, X, a, c))_+ ]
                  + delta_g * (lambda_1 + lambda_2)^T * 1_{K^g}
                  + sum_{c in C} delta_{l,c} * (mu_{1,c} + mu_{2,c})^T *
                    1_{K^{l,c}}                                          (4)

The theorem states: optimality of lambda* provides the global fairness
guarantee, and optimality of mu*_c provides the local fairness guarantee for
client c. (Full proof is in Appendix B, NOT in this PDF.)

Remark given in text: Theorem 1 degenerates to the local-only or global-only
case simply by dropping the unneeded constraint set from Problem (2) — this
is how the paper derives the LoGoFair_l (local-only) and LoGoFair_g
(global-only) ablation variants used in experiments.

Empirical / plug-in estimator version (Eq. 5, page 22475): since the true
eta is unknown, LoGoFair plugs in an estimated eta_hat(x,a,c) (from a
pre-trained probabilistic classifier) and estimated p_hat_{a,c},
phi_hat^{a,c}, psi_hat^{a,c}:

  F_hat(lambda, mu, x, a, c)
    = p_hat_{a,c} * (2*eta_hat(x,a,c) - 1)
      - (lambda_1 - lambda_2)^T * phi_hat^{a,c}(eta_hat(x,a,c))
      - (mu_{1,c} - mu_{2,c})^T * psi_hat^{a,c}(eta_hat(x,a,c))          (5)

Bi-level reformulation (Eq. 6, page 22475): the min_{lambda,mu>=0} H(lambda,
mu) problem is restructured as a bi-level problem so that mu_c can be solved
per-client while only lambda needs federation:

  min_{lambda >= 0} H_hat(lambda) = sum_{c in C} H_hat_c(lambda),
     H_hat_c(lambda) := min_{mu_c >= 0} H_hat_c(lambda, mu_c)            (6)

Per-client local fairness optimization task (Eq. 7, page 22475):

  H_hat_c(lambda, mu_c)
    = sum_{a in A} (1/N^a_{val,c}) * sum_{i=1}^{N^a_{val,c}}
        r_beta( F_hat(lambda, mu, x^a_i,c, a, c) )                       (plus)
      + delta_g/|C| * (lambda_1 + lambda_2)^T * 1_{K^g}
      + delta_{l,c} * (mu_{1,c} + mu_{2,c})^T * 1_{K^{l,c}}              (7)

Where the hinge/ReLU-style term (.)_+ = max(., 0) in Eq. (7)'s underlying
formulation is replaced by a SMOOTHED HINGE (the "logarithmic exponential
relaxation") to make the objective smooth enough for gradient-based
federated optimization (the paper explicitly says raw subgradient descent in
the federated setting "is usually ineffective owing to the intrinsic slow
convergence", citing Yuan, Zaheer, Reddi 2021 on federated composite
optimization). The smoothed hinge is defined (page 22475):

  r_beta(x) = (1/beta) * log(1 + exp(beta*x))

As beta -> infinity, r_beta(x) reduces to max(x, 0). NO NUMERIC VALUE FOR
BETA IS GIVEN ANYWHERE IN THE MAIN TEXT read here. (It may be specified in
Appendix D.1, not present in this PDF — mark as NOT STATED IN AVAILABLE
TEXT / not verifiable from this document.)

Federated optimization procedure: "projection gradient descent is utilized
to approach the optimal solution" for problem (6). The paper states: "We
present the federated post-processing procedure in Algorithm 1 of Appendix
C, along with its efficiency, privacy analysis, and additional discussion."
ALGORITHM 1 ITSELF (the literal pseudocode: exact rounds of client-server
communication, exact projected-gradient-descent update rule/step size for
lambda, inner-loop steps for mu_c, exact stopping criterion) IS IN APPENDIX
C, WHICH IS NOT PRESENT IN THIS PDF. What IS stated in the main body about
the procedure, textually:
  - lambda (global fairness guidance / dual variable) is optimized
    federatively via projected gradient descent across clients, since raw
    client data cannot leave clients and lambda cannot be optimized
    "directly" by any single party.
  - mu_c (local fairness dual variable) is solved purely locally per client
    ("Clients can locally apply subgradient descent... or grid search... to
    enforce local fairness") — i.e., mu_c is a per-client local optimization
    problem, never transmitted/shared in raw form in the sense that it is
    client-specific, local information used only to guarantee that client's
    own local fairness.
  - lambda is described as "global fairness guidance" shared/communicated
    across all clients, and mu_c as the "client-specific parameter" that
    "guarantees local fairness."
  - Communication rounds are denoted T; Figure 3b (Sec 5.5) shows "rapid
    convergence of the three metrics [Acc, M^local_DP, M^global_DP] to
    stable values within 10 rounds across three datasets" — this is the
    paper's explicit statement that communication/post-processing rounds
    converge within ~10 rounds (matches the archive note's expectation of
    "~10" post_rounds, and IS explicitly stated in the main text, not just
    an appendix guess).

Temperature calibration step: "For the purpose of further mitigating the
estimation error in probability scores generated by the FL model, we adopt
model calibration (Guo et al. 2017) to calibrate the learned FL classifier."
(page 22475, Sec 4.3). Guo et al. 2017 "On calibration of modern neural
networks" (ICML) is the canonical temperature-scaling calibration paper —
the paper cites it by name but does NOT give its own temperature-scaling
formula, temperature value, or implementation details; it only says it
"adopts" that calibration technique as a pre-step before post-processing.
This calibration happens on the already-trained probabilistic classifier,
before/alongside estimating eta_hat, p_hat_{a,c}, phi_hat, psi_hat on the
validation set.

min_group_count / small-group safeguard: NOT STATED ANYWHERE IN THE MAIN
TEXT read in this PDF. No explicit "minimum group count" safeguard,
threshold, or fallback-behavior-for-small-groups is mentioned in the 9 pages
available. If this exists, it is implementation detail in Appendix C/D

Validation-set usage (page 22475, Sec 4.3): "we propose to calibrate
fairness via post-processing in a validation dataset D^val = union_{c in C}
D^val_c." Denoting validation data of sensitive group a in client c as
D^val_{a,c} = {x^a_i,c}_{i=1}^{N_{a,c}}, the empirical estimates of F (Eq. 3)
are computed via Eq. (5)/(7) on this validation data. Per Evaluation
Protocols (Sec 5.1, page 22475-22476): "Firstly, we partition each dataset
into a 70% training set and the remaining 30% for test set, while
post-processing models use half of training set as validation set following
previous post-processing works (Xian, Yin, and Zhao 2023; Chen, Klochkov,
and Liu 2024)." So: 70% train / 30% test split overall; of the 70% training
portion, HALF is further carved out as the validation set used for the
post-processing/calibration step (i.e., effectively ~35% of the full dataset
functions as calibration/validation data, ~35% as the portion used to
pretrain/fit the base probabilistic classifier, ~30% held out as test).

  EXPLICIT ANSWER TO THE CIFAR10 QUESTION: CIFAR10 IS NOT MENTIONED ANYWHERE

Mode comparisons (local-only / global-only / local+global): the paper
defines three LoGoFair variants, all derived from the same Theorem 1 by
dropping constraints per the Remark after Theorem 1:
  - LoGoFair_g: global-fairness-only (drops local constraints)
  - LoGoFair_l: local-fairness-only (drops global constraints)
  - LoGoFair_{l&g}: both local AND global fairness simultaneously (the full
    method)
These three rows appear for every (dataset, alpha) combination in Table 1.
Ablation-study takeaway quoted (page 22476): "with a single fairness
constraint, these methods [LoGoFair_l, LoGoFair_g] typically perform better
in the targeted fairness notion compared to LoGoFair_{l&g}... For local
fairness, LoGoFair_l implement[s] fairer federated model in local level
with best accuracy in most cases compared to other methods. Similarly,
LoGoFair_g also outperforms all other methods in balancing accuracy and
global fairness... LoGoFair_{l&g} generally provides both local and global
fairness guarantee at the cost of slight accuracy degradation compared to
LoGoFair_l and LoGoFair_g."

5. OTHER IMPLEMENTATION-RELEVANT DETAILS
-------------------------------------------
- Model-agnosticism: repeatedly emphasized (Introduction contributions list,
  Sec 4.1 Overview, Sec 4.3, Conclusion) as a design goal — LoGoFair is a
  post-processing calibration layer applied AFTER any FL training algorithm
  produces a probabilistic classifier, so it can "be conveniently adopted to
  mitigate bias in a wide range of existing FL algorithms" (page 22473).
  This supports implementing it as a strategy/wrapper rather than a
  from-scratch training algorithm in a codebase.
- Base FL trainer example given by the paper itself: FedAvg (McMahan et al.
  2017), trained with cross-entropy loss, also cited as an example together
  with Tan et al. 2022 ("Towards personalized federated learning") as
  possible personalized-FL alternatives for producing the base probabilistic
  classifier.
- Calibration (Guo et al. 2017, temperature-scaling-style model calibration)
  is applied to the pretrained FL classifier specifically "to further
  mitigate the estimation error in probability scores" before the
  fairness-calibration post-processing step — i.e., there are conceptually
  TWO calibration-like steps: (1) general probability calibration (Guo et
  al. 2017 style, e.g. temperature scaling) to make eta_hat well-calibrated,
  then (2) the fairness-specific lambda/mu post-processing calibration
  (Theorem 1 procedure) on top of that calibrated eta_hat.
- Communication/privacy properties: Sec 4.3 states raw client data "is
  stored on the client side and must remain confidential", so lambda cannot
  be optimized with direct access to any client's raw data — only
  gradient-style/federated information is exchanged for lambda, while mu_c
  (the local-fairness dual variable) is kept and solved ENTIRELY LOCALLY per
  client and is explicitly local, client-specific information. The paper
  promises "efficiency, privacy analysis, and additional discussion" of this
  in Appendix C (not available in this PDF) — so exact message sizes,
  number of communication rounds' worth of payload, or formal privacy
  guarantees (e.g. differential privacy bounds) are NOT stated in the
  available text; only the qualitative claim that lambda-only communication
  (not raw mu_c or raw data) is federated.
- Convex optimization structure: H(lambda) and H_c(lambda, mu_c) are stated
  to be convex (but not smooth) in lambda and mu_c respectively (page
  22475) — the smoothed hinge r_beta is specifically introduced to make the
  per-client objective smooth enough for effective gradient-based federated
  optimization (motivated by citing Yuan, Zaheer, Reddi 2021 on the slow
  convergence of federated composite/subgradient optimization).
- Research questions driving the experiment section (Sec 5, page 22475):
  RQ1 (does LoGoFair beat existing methods on the accuracy/fairness
  balance), RQ2 (can LoGoFair adjust the accuracy vs local/global fairness
  trade-off via delta_l/delta_g — sensitivity analysis), RQ3 (how does
  client count affect LoGoFair performance — scalability), RQ4 (what is
  LoGoFair's communication efficiency).

SUMMARY ANSWER FOR DOWNSTREAM CONFIG-BUILDING (explicit, non-ambiguous):
This paper's empirical section is STRICTLY TABULAR/TEXT-FEATURE
binary-classification fairness benchmarks (Adult, ENEM) plus one face-image
dataset used for its demographic annotation (CelebA / CelebA-Spoof). There
is NO CIFAR10 experiment, no generic image-classification benchmark, and no
multi-class (non-binary) label setting anywhere in this paper. All three
datasets have Y in {0,1} and A in {-1,1} per the paper's own formal setup.
Consequently, any CIFAR10-based reproduction in this codebase (with a
synthetic vehicle-vs-animal proxy sensitive attribute and a binarized
class-0-vs-rest label) is NOT something the paper itself ever tests or
validates — it is a necessary substitution made because CIFAR10 has no real
protected attribute or native binary label, and this divergence from the
paper's actual experimental domain (tabular/face fairness benchmarks with
real sensitive attributes) should be documented explicitly as a limitation
in any thesis write-up, not presented as paper-faithful reproduction of
LoGoFair's own benchmark setting.
