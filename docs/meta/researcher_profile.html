Research spine

Agentic Orchestration for Fair and Robust Federated Learning
Using autonomous agents to dynamically manage fairness constraints, client selection, privacy budgets, and aggregation in FL systems — replacing static protocols with adaptive, agent-based enforcement that responds to runtime conditions.

The core problem: existing FL fairness approaches set constraints at design time. Under distribution shift, heterogeneous clients, and adversarial participation, these constraints fail at runtime. An agentic orchestration layer can observe, reason, and enforce fairness adaptively — treating fairness as a live system property, not a one-time configuration.

Research areas — detailed

Primary — dissertation core
Fair federated learning
Enforcing fairness constraints (demographic parity, equalized odds, group fairness) during FL training across heterogeneous clients with non-IID data distributions. Focus on dynamic enforcement rather than static design-time constraints.
This is my dissertation and primary research identity going into the PhD.
Primary — work experience
Agentic AI systems
Building software that uses autonomous agents to pursue objectives — agent perception, decision-making, tool use, and multi-step planning. Applied experience with LLM-based and rule-based agent architectures in production systems.
Professional background. Differentiates me from pure ML or pure theory researchers.
Secondary — bridge area
Dynamic fairness enforcement
Treating fairness not as a design-time property but as an adaptive runtime guarantee. Fairness violations detected and corrected in real time as data distributions shift, new clients join, or adversarial behaviour emerges.
This is the central contribution of my paper — where agentic AI meets FL fairness.
Secondary — theoretical depth
Privacy-fairness tradeoffs in FL
Differential privacy mechanisms applied to FL often disproportionately harm underrepresented groups, worsening fairness. Dynamic privacy budget allocation per client group can balance both guarantees simultaneously.
Adds theoretical rigour to the systems-level work. Connects to published literature on DP + fairness.
Applied — industry relevance
Trustworthy AI in real-world systems
FL for high-stakes domains — fraud detection, healthcare, personalisation — where fairness is a regulatory requirement (GDPR, EU AI Act). Agentic orchestration makes fairness auditable and enforceable in deployed systems.
Keeps research grounded in real impact. Important for post-PhD industry roles.

FL system architecture

Lets me contribute to systems-oriented research threads where pure ML background is insufficient.

Specific research interests — what excites me

  • Using reinforcement learning agents to learn aggregation policies that optimise for both model performance and fairness metrics simultaneously
  • Multi-agent FL where both server-side and client-side agents coordinate — clients as local fairness guardians, server as global orchestrator
  • Fairness auditability — designing agent-based systems whose fairness decisions are explainable and verifiable after the fact
  • Robustness at the intersection of fairness and security — how do you maintain fairness guarantees when some FL clients are adversarial or corrupted
  • The sociotechnical dimension of FL fairness — how fairness definitions map to real-world harms in specific deployment contexts
  • Applying agentic FL to real industry problems where fairness is not optional — credit scoring, healthcare triage, content moderation
